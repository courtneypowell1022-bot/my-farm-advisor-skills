The analysis asks three questions. Do growers differ in the size and shape of their fields? What does five years of CDL say about crop history, rotation, and field-edge purity? How do heat and rainfall differ across regions and years? A fourth question links shape to CDL edge share.

Headline: field size separates the growers, but field shape does not. Weather separates them strongly. Irregular fields carry more CDL edge noise. Boundary crop labels agree with 2023 CDL on all 30 fields.

Rotation and crop history. Every field has five CDL years (four transitions). Twenty-five fields alternate corn and soybean; one Illinois field (il-r3) and four Nebraska fields (ne-r2, ne-r3, ne-r6, ne-r10) are continuous corn. Nebraska ne-r9 is corn in four of five years. The 2023 snapshot is 16 corn and 14 soybean fields. Nebraska also has the highest non-dominant edge share (mean 3.6%, max 8.5%), versus Illinois (2.7%) and Iowa (1.6%).

Interpretation of the correlation. The negative ρ is consistent with geometry: irregular field outlines have more perimeter per unit area, so a 30 m raster picks up proportionally more mixed edge pixels. Part of the association is therefore mechanical, and it should be used to flag lower CDL class purity — not as evidence that shape causes edge vegetation.

Weather and crop choice. Corn and soybean fields do not differ significantly in local GDD or precipitation within any grower. This is an absence of evidence: each within-grower group has only 3–7 fields and shares nearly identical gridded weather, so the test has low power.

8Assumptions, limitations, and missing data

Assumptions

  • The growing season is 1 April – 31 October. GDD uses base 10 °C and cap 30 °C; a heat day is daily maximum ≥ 35 °C; a wet day is ≥ 1 mm and a dry day is < 1 mm.
  • Field area is planar area in EPSG:5070. Compactness is computed on the field outline as 4πA / P², where 1.0 is a circle.
  • A field’s crop is its CDL dominant class; edge share is the fraction of field pixels outside that class. Dominant shares of 91–99% mean each field is effectively one crop.
  • Weather tests use field-level five-year means, not individual field-years, because fields on one farm share nearly identical gridded values.

Limitations and missing data

  • Small, balanced sample. Three growers and 10 fields each. Grower-level inference rests on 30 fields and 3 groups; within-grower tests have n = 10.
  • CDL is a 30 m classified raster. Dominant class and edge share are resolution-dependent. “Edge” usually means grass, trees, roads, or mixed margins, not a second crop.
  • Weather is gridded (NASA POWER). Fields within a farm share nearly the same values. Between-grower weather comparisons are reliable; within-grower weather–crop comparisons are low-power.
  • Compactness is geometry, not soil compaction. Polsby–Popper measures the field outline only.
  • Year coverage differs. CDL covers 2019–2023; weather covers 2021–2025. Only 2021–2023 overlap, and this build does not model weather–rotation interactions.
  • Agreement is a consistency check. The 30/30 boundary–CDL match is not independent validation, because boundary crop labels may derive from the same CDL source.
  • No missing layers in this build. Input validation passed for every grower, farm, boundary, weather year, CDL year, and required column; the analysis is descriptive, not causal.

Soil analysis was not required and was not the focus. This workflow does not touch SSURGO, SoilGrids, or any soil property. No soil sampling, bulk density, or traffic-compaction analysis was performed. The word “compactness” throughout this report means Polsby–Popper field-shape geometry (4πA / P²), not soil compaction. Imagery, NDVI, terrain, and yield were also out of scope.
